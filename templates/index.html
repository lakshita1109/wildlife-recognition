    Interesting facts

      About the Project

      What it does

      Wildlife AI is a web application for animal and wildlife monitoring. You upload a photo, the server runs it through a neural network, and you get the most likely animals together with confidence scores and a short fact sheet.

      How image classification works

      An image is a grid of pixel values. A convolutional neural network (CNN) passes those pixels through many layers: early layers detect edges and colours, middle layers detect textures and shapes such as fur, feathers or scales, and the final layers combine them into a score for every animal it knows.

      How this model recognises animals

      It uses MobileNetV3, a compact CNN pretrained on the ImageNet dataset (transfer learning). The image is resized to 224×224, normalised, and converted to probabilities with softmax. A first pass recognises the general animal. If it is a cat or a dog, a second model fine-tuned on pet breeds names the breed. If the best probability is too low, the app says it is unsure instead of guessing.

      Datasets used

      Oxford-IIIT Pet Dataset: about 7,300 photos of 37 cat and dog breeds, created by the Visual Geometry Group at the University of Oxford and IIIT Hyderabad. We split it 70% training, 15% validation and 15% testing, and used it to fine-tune the breed model.

      ImageNet: over a million photos in 1,000 classes. The pretrained MobileNetV3 learned from it, and its animal classes are what recognise lions, elephants, birds, reptiles and more.
